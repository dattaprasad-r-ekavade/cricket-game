# Playtest 1 — 7 October 2026

- **Build:** Release, Step 49 working tree (uncommitted ball-follow camera), default settings: 1 over per innings, CPU Standard.
- **Tester:** project owner, keyboard.
- **Scope:** first human play session of the match build.

## Findings

| # | Problem | Impact | Plan task |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The controls can't be learned. About 25 keys, shown as one dense text block on the HUD; no mental model of what to press when. | Blocking | A4a |
| 2 | The batting mechanic isn't understood: when to press, how the shots differ, why a shot hit or missed. | Blocking | A4b |
| 3 | No feedback about the delivery that was bowled (pace, line, length, pitch point) or about the shot (timing, contact quality). | Blocking | A4c |

## Other observations

- The window title still reads "Super Cricket — One Over" although the build plays a two-innings match.

## Next

Implement A4a–A4c from `plan.md`, then repeat the playtest with the same build settings and at least one person who has never seen the game.

## Response in Step 51

The default batting broadcast now frames the pitch at 28 m; the behind-striker view is 21 m and the bowler-end view is 24 m. The game selects the camera for the player role when a delivery begins and switches to ball-follow after bat contact. A persistent last-delivery card reports ball speed, measured pitch length and line, shot/contact quality, runs or wicket, and bowling target error when the player bowled. The Release review captures both batting and bowling versions of that card.

This is code and capture validation, not a human retest of Step 51. Keyboard readability remains to be confirmed, and GamePad play has not been tested.

## Response in Step 52

The batting result card now compares the player's shot-button press against the analyzer-measured best-contact delay for the selected stock delivery and shot. It labels input within 75 ms of that target `PERFECT`; otherwise it reports `EARLY` or `LATE` with the offset in milliseconds. The checked calibration targets cover standard, wide, no-ball, and yorker deliveries, and use the stock delivery identity when CPU bowling adds a variation name. The batting feedback capture visibly shows the new timing label; the bowling result card continues to show target and landing error.

Release build, gameplay checks, calibration-drift checks, 20 live-match trace replays, and screenshot captures pass. This is still code/capture validation: keyboard readability and the usefulness of the timing label have not yet been confirmed by the owner, and GamePad play remains untested.

## Response in Step 53

The default broadcast, behind-striker, and bowler-end cameras now frame the pitch more tightly (22 m, 16 m, and 18 m respectively); the bowler-end aim point is framed centrally. The normal HUD shows `V: camera`, and left-stick click cycles the same views on GamePad. The pause menu and README document both shortcuts. Fresh 1440x900 captures show the target marker and closer player scale, and the full review passes. These captures do not replace an in-game keyboard retest; both keyboard readability and physical GamePad use remain unconfirmed.

## Response in Step 54

The persistent result card now includes a compact pitch map with the bowler's wicket at the top and the batter's wicket at the bottom. A yellow dot shows where the ball actually bounced; when the player bowled, a cyan ring shows the intended point alongside it. The text report still gives the target-to-landing distance. Release captures show the map for both human batting and bowling. Keyboard readability and physical GamePad use remain unconfirmed.

## Response in Step 55

Bat contact now triggers a short quality-scaled flash labelled MIDDLE, CLEAN CONTACT, EDGE CONTACT, THIN CONTACT, or NO CONTACT. The first actual bounce gets a brief expanding pitch ring, and the ball trail transitions from cool colours at lower speed to warm colours at higher speed; the HUD explains the colour cue. Release captures show the contact badge and bounce marker away from the center of play. Automated keyboard-state checks pass at 30, 60, and 120 fps. A human keyboard retest is still needed to confirm timing and readability; GamePad remains untested.
