# Step 58 — Godot stadium and batting presentation trial

**Branch:** `codex/godot-trial`

**Date:** 7 October 2026

**Scope:** Milestone B2. A bounded rendering and asset-import trial; no engine decision.

## Result

Extended the Godot .NET B1 host into a 3D presentation scene while keeping `SuperCricket.Simulation`, `SuperCricket.Content`, and the MonoGame game engine-independent and unchanged by the Godot integration. The scene uses the repository's standard-pace delivery and `BattingPracticeAnalyzer` to calibrate and replay an actual front-foot drive: contact at 0.579 seconds, 0.90 contact quality, then a boundary result. It advances the incoming ball and the 234 recorded outgoing simulation frames at the delivery's fixed 120 Hz timestep.

The Blender practice batter is exported as a 411 KB GLB from `assets/characters/practice-batter.blend`. The repeatable exporter checks for one 13-bone rig and exports all seven actions, including `practice-stance` and `front-foot-drive`. Godot imports and plays those named clips. Stadium geometry is generated by `TrialStadiumBuilder.cs`: oval seating tiers and roof, 1,760 instanced seats, floodlights, sight screens, scoreboard, wickets/creases, boundary rope, and shaded outfield bands.

Three views cover the key moments: a behind-striker camera for the delivery and contact, a high view of the full oval, and a ball-follow view after contact. `C` toggles the role and wide views; `R` restarts the deterministic delivery. The persistent card changes from delivery instruction to shot/timing/contact quality and then the result. The contact and result review captures show the labels legibly against the play image.

The scene uses Godot Forward+ on the desktop trial, 2× MSAA, a four-split shadow-casting directional light, a procedural sky, filmic tone mapping, fog, and glow. Godot's glTF documentation recommends `.gltf`/`.glb` for 3D scenes and notes Blender can export the runtime scene and its animations; the engine's renderer documentation identifies Forward+ as its clustered desktop renderer and documents feature limits in Compatibility. [Godot 3D format/import guidance](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/assets_pipeline/importing_3d_scenes/available_formats.html), [Godot renderer architecture](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/engine_details/architecture/internal_rendering_architecture.html).

## Limits and next step

This proves that the shared delivery and batting simulation can drive a Godot presentation, glTF animation import works, and the needed shadow/post-processing features are available on the chosen desktop renderer. It does not establish Cricket 07 visual fidelity or keyboard playability. The batter is still the 13-bone prototype; the stadium is procedural; there is no bowler animation, completed match, authored stadium asset, performance comparison, or human playtest of the Godot scene.

Milestone B3 remains: compare the inspected Godot captures and development time with the MonoGame build, then record the engine decision. Step 57's revised MonoGame cameras and feedback still await the owner's keyboard retest; GamePad remains untested.
